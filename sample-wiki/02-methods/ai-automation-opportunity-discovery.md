# AI Automation Opportunity Discovery

## Purpose

Identify where AI or automation can deliver value — and where it introduces customer, employee, or operational risk — before committing to solutions.

## Inputs

- Process maps and pain point registers
- Data availability and quality
- Domain context — [[ai-automation-ba]]
- Public research summaries on human–AI interaction

## Steps

1. Start with the **business problem** — [[digital-transformation-framework]].
2. Map the end-to-end domain, not a single bot feature.
3. List candidates: repetitive, rule-based, high-volume steps.
4. Score feasibility: data, integration, exceptions, regulation.
5. Assess human impacts: customers and employees — [[change-impact-analysis]].
6. Define controls: human-in-the-loop, escalation, audit logs.
7. Prioritise by value, risk, adoption effort.
8. Pilot with **behavioural and operational** metrics.

## Outputs

- Automation opportunity register
- Prioritisation matrix
- Use case brief (problem, users, data, controls, metrics)
- Pilot requirements and UAT scenarios including fallback paths

## Common mistakes

- Automating broken processes without future-state redesign
- Measuring only speed, not accuracy or downstream behaviour
- No staff consultation before rollout
- Treating pilot success as production readiness without field validation

## Research-informed example (not a project claim)

Voice ordering AI may require fallback-to-human flows and behavioural KPIs in acceptance criteria. See [[public-paper-ai-service-work-summary]].

## Interview phrasing

> I discover automation opportunities by mapping the domain, scoring repetitive work, and checking customer and employee impacts before we commit to a tool.
